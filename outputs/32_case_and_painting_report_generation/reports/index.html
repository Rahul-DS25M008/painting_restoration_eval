Applicability Boundaries

  • SDXL covers only ten candidates across ten approved cases; it is not treated as a full-population comparison.
  • Repeated-seed uncertainty applies to 115 cases and 165 prompt-specific groups. Deterministic methods are not assigned artificial diffusion uncertainty.
  • The 480 completed N11 p01–p04 context-prompt candidates remain excluded because they lack complete downstream flag and explanation coverage.
  • Candidate observations, repeated seeds, prompt arms, cases, and paintings are nested rather than independent.
  • Metric-specific better or worse results do not create a single overall model verdict.
  • Computational flags support human review and do not replace conservation expertise.
The main limitation is the absence of expert conservation ground truth. Visual and computational agreement can support inspection, but cannot establish historical authenticity or approve treatment.

Visual plausibility is not equivalent to historical or restoration trustworthiness.

Thesis Contribution

The collection supports the thesis through three connected roles:

  • Restoration evaluation: case reports retain classical, perceptual, feature, colour, texture, seam, semantic, spatial, and uncertainty evidence without collapsing them into one score.
  • Method comparison: painting reports show where model behaviour is better, worse, stable, unstable, applicable, or inconclusive within the tested population.
  • Trustworthy decision support: failure categories, flags, explanation maps, limitations, and review actions make the evaluation auditable rather than presenting plausible images alone.
The reporting layer does not decide whether a restoration should be accepted. Its contribution is to make the evidence, disagreements, coverage gaps, and need for expert judgement visible at case, painting, and collection levels.
